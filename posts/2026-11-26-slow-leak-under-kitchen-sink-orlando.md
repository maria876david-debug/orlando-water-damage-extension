# The Slow Leak Under the Kitchen Sink: Orlando's Most Common Hidden Water Damage

It rarely announces itself. No flood, no burst pipe, no water on the floor. Just a slow drip under the kitchen sink — a loose supply line, a worn drain seal, a dishwasher connection that has worked itself free — quietly wetting the cabinet base for weeks. By the time anyone notices, the damage is already done.

## Why the kitchen sink is the usual suspect

The space under a kitchen sink is the busiest plumbing junction in the house: hot and cold supply lines, the drain, the dishwasher hose, sometimes a water filter or a disposal unit — all packed into a dark cabinet nobody opens properly. Every connection is a place a leak can start, and the cabinet hides the first signs. In Orlando's humidity, a damp cabinet base does not dry out between drips; it stays wet, and wet wood plus warmth is exactly what mould needs.

## The signs that give it away

A musty smell when you open the cabinet is the earliest warning, and the easiest to dismiss. After that come the visible signs: the cabinet base swells or softens, the laminate peels, dark staining appears along the back panel, or the floor in front of the sink starts to warp. If the smell is there, the leak is there — even if you cannot see water yet.

## A two-minute check, once a month

Once a month, empty the cabinet and look properly. Run your hand along the supply lines and the drain connections and feel for moisture. Look at the cabinet base for staining or swelling, and check the shut-off valves — turn them once, gently, so you know they still work. Finding the valve that will not turn during an emergency is a bad time to learn it is seized. If anything feels damp, dry it, watch it for a day, and call a professional if it comes back.

## The cost of catching it late

A leak caught in the first week is a washer or a tightened connection. A leak caught after two months is a new cabinet base, damaged flooring, and often mould remediation behind the wall. The difference is one monthly look under the sink.
