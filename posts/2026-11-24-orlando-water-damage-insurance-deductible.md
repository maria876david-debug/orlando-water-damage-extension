# Orlando Water Damage and Your Insurance Deductible: Ask Before You File

After water damage, most Orlando homeowners make the same call in the same order: plumber, then insurer. The insurer call deserves a pause first, because one number in your policy — your deductible — decides whether filing a claim helps you or costs you.

## What a deductible actually is

Your deductible is the amount you pay out of pocket before your insurance starts paying. If your deductible is $1,000 and the repair costs $1,400, the insurer pays $400 — and the claim still goes on your record. If the repair costs less than your deductible, the insurer pays nothing at all, and you've filed a claim for no benefit.

## The question to ask before you file

Get a restoration estimate first, then compare it with your deductible. If the damage is small and the repair cost sits near or below your deductible, paying out of pocket is usually the smarter move. A claim on your record can affect your premiums and your ability to switch insurers later — a real cost that doesn't appear on any repair estimate.

## What your policy actually covers

Not all water damage is covered the same way. A sudden burst pipe is usually covered; a slow leak you've known about for months often isn't, because insurers expect homeowners to maintain their property. Flood water from outside the home is a different matter entirely — standard homeowners policies in Florida typically exclude it, and flood cover comes from a separate policy. Know which kind of water you're dealing with before you describe it to your insurer.

## The evidence that protects your claim

If you do file, your claim is only as strong as your evidence. Photograph the damage before anything is moved, dried or thrown away. Keep the broken part — the burst pipe, the failed valve, the damaged appliance hose — if you can. Keep every receipt from the first day onward, including the emergency call-out. And keep a written timeline: when you found the water, what you did, who you called, and when. Insurers work from records, and the records you make on day one are the ones that matter.

## The order that works

Stop the water. Photograph everything. Get a professional assessment of the damage and the drying needed. Then, with a real estimate in hand, decide whether to file — and call your insurer with the facts, not a guess.
