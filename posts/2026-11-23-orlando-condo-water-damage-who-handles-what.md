# Water Damage in an Orlando Condo: Who Handles What — Owner, Association or Insurer?

A leak in a house has one owner and one insurance policy. A leak in a condo has a unit owner, an association, a master insurance policy, the neighbour below, and a declaration document nobody has read since closing. Sorting out who pays for what — before the drying equipment is even booked — saves the argument that delays half the condo water jobs in Orlando.

## The usual split (check your declaration, because it rules)

In Florida condos, the association's master policy generally covers the building structure and common elements: the roof, exterior walls, shared plumbing in the walls, hallways and lobbies. The unit owner's own policy — an HO-6 — generally covers what's inside the unit: flooring, cabinets, fixtures, personal property, and the damage your leak does to the unit below you, through your liability cover.

Where the line falls on drywall, paint and built-ins varies by community, because each association's declaration draws it. Two condos on the same street can split the same leak differently. The declaration, not a neighbour's memory of the last leak, is the document that decides.

## The three questions to answer in the first day

**Where did the water come from?** A failed shared pipe points toward the association's side. Your washing machine hose points toward yours. The source doesn't settle everything, but it starts the file in the right pile.

**Whose property got wet?** Common elements, your unit, the unit below — each can sit with a different payer. Photograph everything before anything gets moved or dried, in every unit affected, with permission.

**Who needs to know today?** The association (in writing), your own insurer, and the neighbour below. Condo drying often needs access to two units and a shared wall cavity; the association's involvement isn't optional paperwork, it's how the crew gets into the wall.
