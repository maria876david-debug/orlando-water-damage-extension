# What Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Orlando? The Three Tiers, Explained

Ask what water damage restoration costs in Orlando and you will get a range so wide it feels useless — until you understand that the price follows the size and spread of the job, and most jobs fall into one of three tiers. Here is how the tiers on our own pricing break down, and what pushes a job from one into the next.

## Tier one: $1,200–$2,500 — the contained leak

One room, one source, caught early. A supply line under a sink, an AC drain overflow spotted the same day, a water heater leak found before it spread. The work is extraction, drying equipment in one area for a few days, and moisture checks to confirm the structure is dry. This is the tier where speed pays: the same leak found a week later is rarely still in this tier.

## Tier two: $2,800–$5,500 — the leak that travelled

Water that crossed rooms or soaked into walls and flooring: a burst pipe that ran while you were at work, a roof leak that worked through the ceiling into the wall cavities. Now the job includes opening sections of wall or floor to dry what is hidden, removing materials that cannot be saved, and a longer dry-out. Most Orlando homes sit in this tier by the time a hidden leak announces itself with a stain or a smell.

## Tier three: $6,000–$12,000 and up — the major event

Flooding across multiple rooms, storm water through the structure, or damage found after mould has taken hold. These jobs combine full extraction, significant material removal, mould remediation, and staged drying with monitoring. The dry-out itself typically runs 3–5 days in any tier, but the scale of what has to come out and be treated is what moves the price here.

## What moves a job between tiers

Three things: how long the water sat, how far it spread, and what it touched. Every hour of delay works against you, which is why response time matters more than almost anything else — our Orlando crews aim to respond within 45–60 minutes, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. Water damage does not wait for business hours, and neither does the mould clock.
